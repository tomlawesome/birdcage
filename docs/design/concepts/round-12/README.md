# Design round 12 — sign-in, three live concepts (#8, slice B)

2026-11-15. Round 11's verdict: "I strongly dislike all of them." and
"Boring, weak and badly executed concepts." Rounds 8 to 11 all ended as a
form on a dark navy page with thin lines around it, shown as stills.

This round changes the method, not only the drawing:

- Each concept is a working page. Type a password and press Sign in to
  go through the steps; an empty password shows the refusal.
- Each fills the window with one rendered scene (WebGL or canvas), in
  the four network colours, not a decorated card.
- Four states per concept at high finish, not eight scenes: sign in,
  refused, authentication code, and the move into the dashboard. The
  other scenes (first run, enrolment, recovery codes, session expired,
  locked out) are drawn only for the concept that goes forward.
- On-screen copy is plain product wording: "Sign in", "Username",
  "Password", "Incorrect username or password. 4 attempts left.",
  "Authentication code", "Enter the 6-digit code from your authenticator
  app.", "Continue", "Use a passkey", "Single sign-on", "Use a recovery
  code".

Question 18 (start from round 9's smoked glass, or a clean sheet) was not
answered. Decided here, open to overrule: one concept takes the glass
further (AB) and two are new (AC, AD).

## Shared rules

Unchanged from earlier rounds: nothing the cage has seen is shown before
sign-in; no account is named but the visitor's own; refusal is never
alarm red; the main button is the only filled control (4a); shell colours
and type are the ratified ones (ADR-0004). With reduced motion set, each
scene holds still and the steps cut instead of moving.

## The concepts

| | Name | Scene | Code step | Into the dashboard |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| AB | Reeded glass | A wall of fluted glass with coloured light drifting behind it. The form is on a clear smoked panel at the left. | The panel slides to the right of centre. | The wall parts in the middle like two sliding doors. |
| AC | Ribbons | Four silk ribbons, one per network colour, turning slowly. Typing sends a pulse of light along them. | The ribbons draw together and twist tighter. | The ribbons flatten into the dashboard's four traces, in place. |

Refused, in all three: the scene loses its colour and dims; the message
sits under the password field. Colour returns on the next keystroke.

## Cost to build

- AB and AC are one fragment shader each on a full-window WebGL canvas;
  AD is a 2D canvas. No library, no image assets, about 100 lines each.
- A browser without WebGL needs a fallback for AB and AC: a still
  gradient in the same colours. Not drawn yet.
- The prototypes load a picture of the dashboard (`dashboard.jpg`) to
  show the hand-over. A real build must not load anything from the
  dashboard before sign-in; the scene would finish on the void and the
  dashboard would then load.
- The CSP needs no change: the scripts would ship as files, not inline.

## Files

- `direction-ab-reeded-glass.html`, `direction-ac-ribbons.html`,
  `direction-ad-the-dome.html`: generated by `python3 -I gen.py` from
  `src/`. Edit `src/`, not the pages.
- `capture.mjs`: retakes `shots/` from the design host (needs the
  software WebGL flags it sets). `shot.mjs`: one frame, for checking.
- The "entering" shot is a frame set by hand part-way through the move;
  the others are the pages as they run.
