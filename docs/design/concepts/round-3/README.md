# Design round 3 — dashboard shell (#3)

Opened by the round-2 verdict (owner, 2026-09-12, verbatim): **"The docket
is closer. You don't have to just reuse the same graphing and stuff, it's
the essence of them that's important. Think outside the box, generate two
more twists on the docket version."**

Round 3 keeps what the docket got right — free chrome on the void, colour
as meaning, a stripe and a sentence per visitor, raw OpenCanary lines as
evidence, pill actions, the amber brink as "now" — and drops mikroview's
actual charts. Neither direction has a fall, a ribbon or a sparkline.

## Directions

| Letter | Name | Landing | Twist |
|-------:|------|---------|-------|
| F | **The night book** | the book | The docket as a watch-log. No table, no chart: dated headings (`TONIGHT · FRI 12 SEP`), one entry per visitor newest first with a mono time gutter, a kind chip, a sentence, the raw lines beneath, gaps written as words ("The twelve days before that: quiet"). Looking something up rewrites the book to its entries only. Canaries keep a roll on the right. |
| G | **The score** | the score | The docket rows keep stripe + sentence, and each row owns a stave on one shared time axis that stretches the last 15 min and squeezes the fortnight (`14 d · 24 h · 1 h · 15 m · NOW`). Dual colour: a tick on a visitor's stave is coloured by the canary it touched; a tick on a canary's stave by the visitor kind. Heartbeats are dim ticks where the axis is stretched. Opening the sweep splits its stave into one line per canary. |

## Scenes (both directions prove all three)

1. landing — `f-book` (tonight, 24 h) / `g-score` (14 d)
2. one address looked up: `203.0.113.42` — `f-lookup` / `g-lookup`
   (G switches to a linear last hour with words under every tick)
3. the sweep opened with story, lines and actions — `f-open` / `g-open`
